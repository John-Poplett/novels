# Dataset changelog

This tracks changes to the *dataset itself* — what a `tar_read(novels)` produces — separately from ordinary code changes to the pipeline. Each entry only changes one thing about what "success" means or what data backs it, so a version can be adopted independently of the others.

## Unreleased (v1.2.0, "2013+")

- The three LibriVox rows patched in 1.1.0 now carry their text editions' download counts, not the audio entries'. They keep the audio entry's id as their key, so they had kept its count too. Each text edition's count is read from the Wayback Machine's capture of its page nearest the scrape (5 November 2012; PREREGISTRATION.md, amendment of 2026-11-25), in `inst/extdata/librivox_counts.csv` with the capture's URL; `applyLibrivoxCounts()` re-derives each label from its genre's band, measured on the genre's other rows, and refuses a count between the bands.
  - 19513, *A Journey to the Center of the Earth* (Adventure): 336 → 1,197 (text edition 18857, 2012-11-04). Still a success (Adventure successes are at least 100).
  - 19640, *Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* (Adventure): 1,092 → 17,305 (76, 2012-10-18). Still a success.
  - **19836, *The Verse-Book of a Homely Woman* (Poetry): 9 → 78 (3477, 2012-11-02), and its label flips from failure to success** (Poetry successes are at least 70, failures at most 9). The row is Fay Inchfawn's *Verse-Book* in its own right: the book the 2013 authors sampled under this id, her *Early Spring*, has no text edition, so the 1.1.0 patch put the *Verse-Book*'s text in its place, and its count and label are now the *Verse-Book*'s too. 78 is near Poetry's line of 70 (the page read 212 and 121 in May 2012), so this is the one label of the three that depends on which reading is used.
  *Effect on results:* against the corpus with the two recovered rows and the old counts, the pooled sweep moved by −0.005 to +0.008 (largest at `alpha = 0.03`), inside one standard error at every `alpha`. The model reads the label, not the count, so the only change it sees is 19836's flip; no author is added, so the inner folds are unchanged and the movement is that one book's.

- Recovered the two books the 2013 metadata lost, both Short Stories failures in fold 5 whose texts were always in the archive (`Short_Stories/ss_fold5/failure5/`): 23177, *The Inmate of the Dungeon* (W. C. Morrow, 1894), whose line in `novel_meta.txt` breaks off before its author and count, and 23178, *The Indian's Hand* (Lorimer Stoddard, 1892), the archive's 800th file, with no line at all. Genre, fold and class are the folder's, as the 2013 authors filed them; title, author and language come from the catalogue join, as for every row; the count is the Wayback Machine's capture of the book's page nearest the scrape, 2012-11-03 for both (5 November 2012; PREREGISTRATION.md, amendment of 2026-11-25). `inst/extdata/recovered_rows.csv` holds them with each capture's URL, and `recoverLostRows()` checks every row against its folder before adding it. 23178 reads 4, inside Short Stories' failure band (at most 9). 23177 reads 10, one above it; captures two days before the scrape read within ±1 of the corpus for 86% of books with counts of 30 or less, so it keeps the folder's label (decided 2026-11-25). The two authors are new to the author-fold table, appended by the balance rule (`inst/extdata/README.md`): Morrow, W. C. to fold 5, Stoddard, Lorimer to fold 3. 796 rows to 798, 754 books to 756.
  *Effect on results:* the pooled sweep moved by +0.002 to −0.012, inside one standard error at every `alpha`. Adding two authors also reshuffles the inner, author-grouped folds `cv.glmnet` tunes on (42–54% of training rows moved, by outer fold), so the movement is split in two (`data-raw/recovery-effect.R`: the same chain run with every existing author's inner fold held where it was, after checking that the run without the books reproduces the stored baseline exactly). **The books** lowered the AUC at every `alpha`, by 0.003 to 0.015; **the reshuffle** moved it by −0.008 to +0.010, either way, as tuning noise should. At `alpha = 1` the −0.012 is −0.004 from the books and −0.008 from the reshuffle.

- Stripped the pulp-magazine transcriber's note from the end of 73 books (`stripTranscriberNote()`): "transcriber's note: this etext was produced from _astounding science fiction_ march 1959. extensive research did not uncover any evidence that the u.s. copyright on this publication was renewed. minor spelling and typographical errors have been corrected without note." It records where the transcription came from, not what the author wrote, and it leaked the label: in Short Stories all 11 books carrying it were successes and none of the failures (Fisher p = 0.0005), eight of them pulp stories also in Science Fiction, where it was evenly split (35 failures, 34 successes). 20,080 characters removed, 182–371 per book, each a cut of the book's tail at the note's header line; `tidyRawData()` asserts that no copy survives.
  *Effect on results:* the pooled sweep moved by at most 0.0025 at any `alpha`, inside one standard error everywhere. Within Short Stories, scored from the pooled model's out-of-fold predictions at `alpha = 0.05` (`data-raw/transcriber-note-effect.R`), the AUC fell from 0.719 to 0.708 (−0.012): the direction leakage predicts, and well inside the noise of 98 rows. It is removed because it is provenance, not style, not because of its size. Rows, labels, counts and folds are unchanged; the corpus fingerprint is now `eb7fabad40da4fb1056d73d9556222e0`.

## v1.1.0 — source-integrity fix (2026-11-21)

Same titles, genres, fold structure, and success/failure labels as the original Ashok, Feng & Choi (2013) corpus. No relabeling. Text-source corrections only, plus two rows dropped as duplicates (below):

- Fixed a join bug that inflated 798 correct book observations to 884 by cross-multiplying genre-directory file copies against genre-specific metadata rows.
- Fixed 5 books whose `gutenberg_id` pointed at a LibriVox audio-recording catalog entry instead of the text edition. Three (`19513`, `19640`, `19836`) have their text replaced with a real edition from a different id; `19836`'s title is corrected, because *The Verse-Book of a Homely Woman* stands in for the unavailable collection *Early Spring*. The other two (`19678`, `19782`) are **dropped**, because the real books are already in the corpus as `215` and `157`. The dataset is now 796 rows over 754 books (was 798 over 756). The `download_count` and label of the three patched rows are still the audio entries' pending a refresh. An earlier version of this fix re-downloaded the contaminated ids and changed nothing; see `librivox_replacements` and `librivox_drops` in `R/targetFunctions.R`.
  *Effect on results:* mean test AUC rose by 0.002–0.015 at every `alpha` (mean +0.0085), e.g. 0.739 → 0.744 at `alpha = 0.01` and 0.686 → 0.695 at `alpha = 1`. No `alpha` moved by more than two standard errors, so this is within fold-to-fold noise and is not claimed as an improvement. Only 2 of 514 authors changed fold. Old and new baselines are in `baselines/`.
- Added `inst/extdata/author_folds.csv`: the author-grouped fold assignment (`author5_v1`: seed 42, five folds) stored as data, one row per author, so any language can reproduce the folds. It is the assignment the pipeline already used, so all baselines stay valid, and it is append-only (a positional seed reshuffles 75–80% of existing authors whenever the author count changes). `novels` itself is unchanged.
- Stripped Project Gutenberg license header/footer boilerplate from 48 of the 754 books, in three shapes the previous stripper missed because it required asterisks on both markers: an unstarred `end of the project gutenberg ebook of <title>, by <author>` line sitting just above the starred end marker (37 books, so it became the body's last line), the possessive `end of project gutenberg's <title>, by <author>` (11 books), and a production credit placed after the start marker rather than before it (19 books; several books carry more than one shape). 5,293 characters removed (0.002% of the corpus; median 101 per changed book, max 204). A book's own title page is kept: header stripping consumes only a leading run of machine-generated lines, and text that merely mentions Project Gutenberg mid-line is left alone (3 books still do, 2 of them in editorial notes).
  *Effect on results:* mean test AUC moved by at most 0.0025 at any `alpha` (mean −0.0001), inside one standard error everywhere, so this is not claimed as an improvement. Rows, books, labels, download counts and folds are unchanged; baselines were regenerated and the corpus fingerprint is now `4cea80937cee9b4c8a1151717d7d4ff8`.
- Removed the dataset's static `fold` column from downstream use; CV fold assignments are now generated fresh, from a documented, seeded function, rather than trusted as opaque shipped state. (The original `fold` column is still present in parsed metadata for anyone who wants to reproduce the original paper's exact published per-genre CV numbers on the unmodified corpus.)

## v1.0 — original corpus, as parsed

The Ashok, Feng & Choi (2013) EMNLP corpus, faithfully parsed from its original hierarchical metadata format, with no text-source corrections applied. Known to contain the issues fixed in v1.1.
